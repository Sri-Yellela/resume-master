## BULLET CRAFT

Start from the candidate's own bullets. Reword them for this posting first, keeping their numbers and specifics. Write a new bullet only for a must-have posting term that the candidate's own work at that employer genuinely covers.

The shape of a bullet, in this order:
1. a strong past-tense verb (present tense for a current role), the posting's own verbs first, else the domain verb pool;
2. what was built, changed or delivered;
3. with which skill or tool, tied to a decision or to what it made possible, never namedropped;
4. the result;
5. why it mattered: to users, to the business, or to the team.

One accomplishment per bullet, at most 30 words. No "Responsible for", "Worked on", or noun-led bullets.

Quantifiers: keep every number the candidate gave, exactly, and bring it forward into the result. A bullet with no number states its scope or outcome in words from the candidate's own material ("for all checkout traffic", "across the team's services"). Never a new number: no new metric, count, percentage, team size, year count or duration.

Contribution: use Built, Implemented, Contributed to, Co-designed or Collaborated with unless the base resume itself states sole ownership. Never Led, Owned, Spearheaded or Single-handedly unless the candidate's own bullet says so.

Order for relevance: within each employer, the bullet most relevant to the target posting comes first (the one carrying the most must-have terms). Employers stay in date order. Skills rows lead with what the posting stresses; projects are ordered the same way.

Variety, as a preference and never a quota: no more than two bullets on the same arc. Where the candidate's material supports it, one bullet shows a stakeholder or business judgment and one shows a trade-off and its outcome. Never invent either.

Wording: use posting keywords verbatim (the exact spelling and casing of the keyword list), each where it honestly describes the work. Bold posting keywords, tools and the candidate's own numbers with **double asterisks**, never a whole bullet.

Banned: em dashes and en dashes (technical hyphens are fine); filler phrases (fast-paced environment, cross-functional collaboration, end-to-end, best practices, state-of-the-art, cutting-edge, world-class, synergy, leverage); filler adjectives (Dynamic, Passionate, Proven, Results-driven, Detail-oriented, Dedicated, Hardworking, Motivated, or equivalents).

<!--IF:SUMMARY-->## SUMMARY

SUMMARY appears immediately after header. 430-480 rendered characters, 3-4 compact sentences, present tense, no metrics, no education, no filler adjectives, no target-company marketing copy. Open with the candidate's role title and total years of experience. Name two or three posting-relevant strengths the candidate has. Close with a sentence on the role's function and the candidate's fit for it.

The years figure comes from `Candidate years of experience` in the runtime inputs, or from the base resume when that is absent. Never from the JD. If the JD asks for more years than the candidate has, the honest figure still goes in the summary — a requirement is what the employer wants, not a fact about the candidate, and it is theirs to weigh. Do not write a range, "N+", or a vaguer phrasing to blur the gap, and do not omit the figure to avoid stating it.

The opening title is the candidate's own level, not the JD's. Their level is `Seniority the candidate states they are` in the runtime inputs, or what the base resume evidences — take whichever is higher, and never go above it. If the JD is for a Senior/Staff/Principal/Lead role and neither supports that level, open with the unqualified role ("Software Engineer", not "Senior Software Engineer"). Applying above your stated level is legitimate and the candidate's decision; claiming it in the resume is not.<!--ENDIF-->
